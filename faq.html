About MLG

8 questions
What is the Munich Leadership Group and what do you do?

The Munich Leadership Group (MLG) is an international leadership consultancy, founded in 2008 and headquartered in Munich. We build the cognitive and emotional operating system of leadership: leadership development, coaching & sparring, audits & assessments, and cultural transformation. More than 50 experts from 10+ countries have so far delivered tailor-made programs to 182,000+ participants at 80+ ambitious clients, from large corporations to midsize companies, start-ups and scale-ups.

What makes MLG different from other leadership consultancies?

Three things: our work is evidence-based and deeply rooted in science (psychology and neuroscience), it is fully customized (we offer no off-the-shelf products), and it works in practice, since we co-develop every activity together with our clients. We are not the polished slide-deck consultancy: clients tell us we are human, flexible, no-nonsense, straight to the point, and that we go the extra mile. And we don’t sell man-days. We do exactly what is needed, step back, and return when you need us — our goal is to enable you to solve problems yourself.

What does "science-based leadership development" mean in practice?

Every design decision follows how people actually learn and change: habit formation instead of one-off knowledge dumps, psychological safety so real issues surface, and curiosity as the door-opener for new behavior. We ground our work in current psychology and neuroscience research, exchange with institutions such as the Max-Planck-Institute, Berkeley, Harvard, Hult Ashridge and Oxford Saïd Business School, and we are independent, bound by no dogma: we do what works.

How is neuroscience used in your programs?

Our Managing Partner Dr. Hans W. Hagemann co-authored "The Leading Brain", the internationally acclaimed best-selling book that translates neuroscience into practical leadership strategies — focus, emotion regulation, decision-making, and peak performance. Those same principles shape our programs: we design for psychological safety, cognitive peak performance, and habit change, and we use creative tools that trigger curiosity, because a curious brain is an open brain.

Who are the experts behind MLG?

A hand-selected core team in Munich, led by Managing Partners Dr. Hans W. Our coaches are ICF-certified by institutions such as Erickson Coaching International and CTI, and they also work as facilitators and consultants on MLG projects — giving them a true 360° view of leadership reality. You can meet the whole team on our website.

Services & Approach

9 questions
Which services does MLG offer?

Four families, all fully customized: Leadership Development (learning journeys, team development, talent management, and the open-enrolment MLG Campus), Coaching & Sparring (performance, development, and transition coaching plus executive sparring), Audits & Assessments (including diagnostic tools and psychometrics), and Cultural Transformation.

What’s the difference between Leadership Development, Coaching & Sparring, and Team Development?

Leadership Development builds capability at scale: programs and journeys for cohorts of leaders. Coaching & Sparring is individual work, one-on-one with an MLG expert. Team Development focuses on one working team — diagnostics, workshops, and deep dives that get the best out of everyone. In practice, the strongest results often combine all three.

What is a "Learning Journey" and how long does it take?

A Learning Journey is a series of designed development activities and formats over time, not an event. It blends face-to-face modules, virtual deep-dives, psychometric tools, curated company visits, learning partnerships, coaching, networking, and a digital learning platform. Typical journeys run from several months to over a year, sized anywhere from 25 top executives to 300 senior leaders. The reason: knowledge is not the real bottleneck — application is, and turning learning into behavior requires practice with feedback loops over time.

What is the difference between coaching and "sparring"?

Coaching is a structured development process with an ICF-certified coach, built around your goals. Executive Sparring is a reflection partnership on eye-level for top executives: concrete critical situations, discussed with an expert who understands both your business and your "human system" — delivered in sessions of different lengths, flexible, discreet, with a personal hotline on top.

When should we choose Performance, Development, or Transition Coaching?

Performance Coaching when the current role demands everything: energy management, effectiveness, unleashing hidden potential. Development Coaching when your scope is growing and the next step brings new challenges: value conflicts, power dynamics, delegation, networking. Transition Coaching when you enter a new role or environment and want to perform from day one — including the first 100 days.

What does Cultural Transformation involve?

Looking into the future. Understanding the scenario and the overall target. Translating it into an inspiring vision. Getting everyone engaged. Finding and particularly supporting those who are (or will be) in key roles to develop them into true role models. Every transformation we run is a well-curated set of cultural goals, strong commitment from the top, personal habit changes, role-model behavior, and what we call "guerrilla activities" that spark curiosity and movement. The magic lies in combining neuroscience with creative tools. We have done this for entire corporations, Mittelstand companies, and single departments, typically when radical new behaviors, restructurings, disruptions or a new strategic direction that needs full company leadership alignment are on the agenda.

What is Talent Management at MLG and how do you identify high potentials?

For us it is not about judging people: it is about precisely recognizing strengths and development opportunities, checking cultural fit, and then developing talents in the best possible way. We combine modern diagnostics, insights from psychology and neuroscience, and years of hands-on experience: talent identification, high-potential programs, structured talent reviews, (reverse) mentoring, and Development Centers. Because the best talents don’t want to be managed — they want to be seen, and want to grow.

What are Audits & Assessments / Development Centers, and how are they different from classic assessments?

They are not "tests" and not keep-or-fire instruments. They give individuals sound feedback on their unique strengths and development potentials, and give you confident talent decisions, reduced hiring risk, and stronger succession pipelines. After diligent preparation we co-develop the process with you, combining diagnostic expertise, latest psychometric tools, customized case studies, and stand-up challenges. We work openly with every candidate, with candid feedback and strict confidentiality — never with hidden agendas or messaging behind anyone’s back. Our Development Centers serve development, not selection.

Which diagnostic tools and psychometrics do you use (e.g. Hogan, CliftonStrengths, NeuroColor, Zortify)?

We are licensed in many psychometric instruments and have focused on four that passed our quality standards: CliftonStrengths®, NeuroColor™, Zortify®, and Hogan®. We stay in close touch with the researchers behind these tools, continuously screening new developments (AI is accelerating the field rapidly), and matching the right instrument to your specific purpose.

Differentiation & Value

6 questions
Why should we pay for MLG instead of using cheaper trainers or free online content?

Content is free everywhere — impactful application is the bottleneck. What you pay for is customization to your exact situation, senior facilitators who create psychological safety fast enough that the real issues surface, and designs that turn insight into behavior. An off-the-shelf training is cheaper on the invoice and more expensive in wasted leadership time.

Why work with MLG instead of just using AI?

AI is genuinely useful for leadership: preparing decisions, structuring information, even rehearsing a difficult conversation. What it does not do is change behavior. Knowing what good leadership looks like was never the bottleneck; doing it under pressure is. Behavior change needs practice, honest feedback from people whose judgment you trust, and an environment safe enough to try and fail. Those are social conditions, not information problems. Leadership is also a deeply human undertaking. People are social beings: they sense whether you mean it, they follow those they trust, and team performance rests on relationships, psychological safety and shared purpose. An AI can suggest what to say. It cannot create the trust that makes a team believe it, and it cannot carry a room through a hard conversation. So we use AI where it helps and work with people where it counts. The more AI absorbs the analytical work, the more the human part of leadership becomes the real competitive advantage.

What can you do that our internal HR/L&D team can’t?

We complement your team and collaborate with it; we don’t replace it. What we add: external candor (we can say what insiders can’t), credibility with top executives, a bench of 50+ specialists across topics, languages, and regions, cutting-edge diagnostics, and pattern recognition from thousands of programs across industries. Your HR and L&D leaders keep ownership. We complement their experience with our outside perspective and make the mutual project most effective.

Everyone claims to be "customized" and "practical" — how are you genuinely different?

The difference is degree: we have no standard product to protect, so nothing pushes us toward templates. Talent varies, but leadership behavior is learnable, and the neuroscience is clear that adult brains build new habits throughout life. Fit & Risk

5 questions
We’ve tried leadership programs before and were disappointed — why would this be different?

Most disappointments have the same anatomy: generic content, no psychological safety, too little involvement of internal role models, no transfer. We start by understanding exactly why the last attempt failed, then design against it: your real situations instead of textbook cases, an atmosphere where people address the true problems within hours, using internal role models for credibility, and habit-level follow-through. We’re happy to show you concrete designs and connect you with references who were skeptical too.

Our leaders are already overloaded — how much time will this really take?

We design for full calendars: focused modules, virtual deep-dives, coaching that fits between meetings, and we make exactly the effort that is needed, never man-day padding. But let’s be honest about the alternative: business as usual under growing pressure doesn’t save time, it compounds the problem. The hours invested come back as better decisions and faster execution.

How do you handle skeptical or resistant senior leaders?

We enjoy them — skepticism usually means experience with bad programs. We meet senior leaders at eye level, with vulnerability instead of a "we know everything" attitude, and we win them through curiosity: a surprising claim, a piece of science, a case that mirrors their reality. Top executives are our core audience, and converted skeptics are our best references.

What if our culture or industry is very specific — do you really understand our world?

We have worked across virtually every industry since 2008, thousands of programs, 182,000+ participants, and we still never pretend to know your world better than you do. That’s exactly why our process starts with diligent preparation and co-creation: your specifics shape the design. Clients across sectors tell us we "fully understand our culture and the business we are in", because we made understanding it the first step.

Is coaching confidential, or does leadership get a report on what was discussed?

Confidential. Full stop. We never report individual coaching content to leadership: using participants’ trust to brief their superiors is on our list of things we will not do at any price. The coaching contract defines the few formal touchpoints (e.g., jointly agreed development goals), and whatever is shared there comes from the coachee, not from us.
